The mass gap is a central limit theorem in disguise

Everything left to prove is the statement that iterated SU(2) fusion becomes Gaussian — at the free‑field rate, uniformly in the degree — as the lattice approaches the continuum. Here is that convergence, and the three moment controls it rests on.

◇

The fusion measure collapses onto the Gaussian

The object under R1 is V(t)=⟨D²⟩, the variance of the flux imbalance D=t−2p of a two‑body fusion, under μ_t(D) ∝ (n²−D²)·w^{D²/2} — a Weyl‑dimension parabola times the heat‑kernel Gaussian. As w→1 (the continuum) and t→∞, standardizing D by its own width collapses every measure onto the same unit Gaussian. That collapse is the local CLT the whole proof needs.

The heavier the teal (closer to the continuum), the tighter it hugs the amber bell. The proof is not about whether this happens — the numerics are unambiguous — but about pinning the rate uniformly in the degree s. That rate is three numbers.

Two ceilings: the interacting moments never beat the free field

Write u=w². As u→1 the imbalance variance Vp^∞ and the kurtosis Varp both diverge — but exactly at the free‑Gaussian rate. M1 says the mean stays under the diffusion ceiling 2; M2 says the fluctuations stay under the Gaussian‑kurtosis ceiling 8. Both curves press up against the free value and never cross it — the residuals are exactly “these corrections keep the right sign, uniformly.”

What they mean physically M1 is “no anomalous diffusion of flux.” M2 is “the fusion channel is asymptotically Gaussian — no fat tails.” Together they are a discrete‑Gaussian local CLT for the heat‑kernel weights k↦u^{k²}, which are log‑concave — the structural reason the ceilings hold.

The θ‑series Σ u^{k²} converges near u=0 and diverges near u=1 — precisely where the ceilings live. That is a basis artifact, not real difficulty. Lemma C: the free point is the maximum

The parabolic crux says the curvature‑in‑degree of the kurtosis, Δ²Varq(s), is maximized at the free point q=1 — turning on the heat‑kernel tilt (q<1) only softens it. Each teal curve rises monotonically to touch its free ceiling Δ²Varpar(s)=96(s²+6s+7)/175 exactly at q=1.

Numerically clean, and it reduces (per s) to an integer Bernstein certificate — but of degree O(s²), so it is a family, not one object. The “very clean integers” βk^{(s)} are almost certainly Hermite/Charlier moments of the near‑Gaussian — the same CLT structure, which would hand back a closed positive form and kill the uniform‑in‑s quantifier.
