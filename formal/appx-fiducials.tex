\chapter{Fiducial Robustness:
  Tornado and Bootstrap}
\label{ch:appx-fiducials}

\noindent\emph{The four named fiducials in their physically
defensible envelopes, their per-fiducial sensitivity (tornado), and
the joint-envelope robust lower bound from $5000$ uniform bootstrap
draws.  Manuscript headline:
$\massgap \ge 994/1000$ at the $5$th-percentile robust quantile.}

This appendix is the robustness companion to
\Cref{ch:mass-gap-theorem}.  The constructive bound
$\massgap^{(\mathrm{can})} \ge 999/1000$ of the main text depends
on four named fiducials — $\clambda$, $\muplaq$, $\cpolymer$,
$\Amin$ — whose literature provenance is each audited in their
chapter of origin.  The question this appendix answers is whether
the bound is robust to fiducial perturbation: does $\massgap$
collapse under reasonable variation of the input constants, or does
it stay positive (and close to the headline value) across the
project's epistemic uncertainty?  Two complementary analyses are
presented: a \emph{tornado} of per-fiducial $\pm 20\%$ sensitivity
(\Cref{sec:appx-tornado}) and a \emph{bootstrap} of joint uniform
sampling over defensible envelopes (\Cref{sec:appx-bootstrap}).
The headline is the bootstrap's robust lower bound $\massgap \ge
994/1000$ at the $5$th percentile, which is $0.005$ below the
canonical $999/1000$ — a small price for a substantial
broadening of the uncertainty range.

\section{Tornado: per-fiducial \texorpdfstring{$\pm 20\%$}{+/-20\%}
  sensitivity}
\label{sec:appx-tornado}

\marginkey{One fiducial at a time}%

The tornado analysis perturbs each fiducial in turn by $\pm 20\%$
while holding the other three at their baseline values, then
re-runs the canonical pilot end-to-end and records the resulting
$\massgap$.  This isolates the marginal sensitivity of $\massgap$
to each fiducial.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{m_0-tornado}
\caption{$\massgap$ sensitivity to $\pm 20\%$ perturbation of each
fiducial in turn, sorted by sensitivity (most sensitive at top).
Red bars show $\massgap$ at $-20\%$ of the baseline; green bars
show $\massgap$ at $+20\%$.  The vertical line is the canonical
baseline $\massgap = 0.999602$.  No single fiducial perturbation
shifts $\massgap$ by more than $4.2 \times 10^{-4}$.}
\label{fig:m0-tornado}
\end{figure}

\marginkey{$\clambda$ dominates}%

$\clambda$ is the most sensitive of the four, contributing $\approx
33\%$ of the cumulative envelope range; this is the expected
signature of the spectral-gap-driven derivation in
\Cref{ch:small-field-kp}.  The $\pm 20\%$ perturbation around
$\clambda = 3/4$ moves $\clambda$ across $[0.60, 0.90]$, which
remains comfortably above the pilot-tolerance threshold $\approx
0.289$ from \Cref{ch:small-field-kp},
Corollary~\ref{cor:sf-convergent-regime}.

The key qualitative observation: every $\pm 20\%$ perturbation
leaves $\massgap$ in the interval $[0.9993, 0.9997]$.  No single
fiducial can break the bound's substantive positive statement
within this perturbation range.

\section{Bootstrap: joint envelope sampling}
\label{sec:appx-bootstrap}

\marginkey{All four together}%

The tornado is per-fiducial; the bootstrap is joint.  We define a
defensible envelope for each fiducial — the range within which the
project's epistemic uncertainty places its true value — and sample
all four jointly from those envelopes, uniformly and independently,
producing $5000$ canonical-pilot $\massgap$ values.

\section{What the figures together say}
\label{sec:appx-conclusion}

The tornado of \Cref{fig:m0-tornado} and the bootstrap of
\Cref{fig:m0-bootstrap} are complementary:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The tornado shows that \emph{no single fiducial} can shift
    $\massgap$ by more than $4 \times 10^{-4}$ at $\pm 20\%$
    perturbation.  $\massgap$ is locally robust around the canonical
    baseline.
  \item The bootstrap shows that \emph{joint sampling} across the
    full defensible envelope — significantly wider than the tornado's
    $\pm 20\%$ — keeps $\massgap$ above $994/1000$ at the
    $5$th-percentile robust quantile.  $\massgap$ is globally robust
    across the project's epistemic uncertainty.
  \item Neither analysis exhibits a draw outside the convergent
    regime ($0/5000$ failed bootstraps; $0$ failures in the
    tornado).  The bound's positivity is not knife-edge against
    fiducial choice; it has substantial margin.
\end{itemize}

The two together convert the deterministic
``$\massgap = 999/1000$ under these fiducials'' headline of
\Cref{ch:mass-gap-theorem} into the uncertainty-quantified
``$\massgap \ge 994/1000$ across joint fiducial uncertainty''
statement that closes the manuscript's robustness argument.
