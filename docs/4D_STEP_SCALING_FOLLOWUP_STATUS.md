### Stage E result (final, 2026-10-17 23:15 PDT) and ERRATUM on the two-loop reference
**Erratum (found 23:15 PDT while finalising E).** In the Stage C text and in the 22:40 partial E note above I used a first-order two-loop reference with b1 = 34/(3 (16 pi^2)^2), i.e. missing the SU(N) factor N^2 = 4. The correct SU(2), N_f=0 two-loop coefficient is b1 = (136/3)/(16 pi^2)^2 = 0.001818 (matches `B2 = 136/3` in `stepscale.py`). With d(1/g2)/dlnL = -(2 b0 + 2 b1 g2) integrated exactly over L -> 2L, the two-loop step-scaling shift s = 1/u - 1/Sigma is: u=1.54: 0.0685, 1.6: 0.0686, 2.0: 0.0698, 2.35: 0.0708, 2.4: 0.0710, 2.5: 0.0714 (one-loop: 0.0644). So the two-loop shift exceeds one-loop by 6% (u~1.5) to 10% (u~2.4), not by 1.5–2.3% as I wrote. (The truncated series `sigma_twoloop` in `stepscale.py` and the statement in `4D_STEP_SCALING_RESULTS.md` that one-/two-loop differ by < 1.1% refer to Sigma, which differs by <~2% — in the shift 1/u - 1/Sigma, which is what is measured, the difference is 6–10%, and the truncated u^3 series underestimates it: 0.0674 vs 0.0710 at u=2.4.) Consequently the Stage C statement "data cannot discriminate one-loop from two-loop" and the 'consistent with two-loop-like 0.0654–0.0659' wording are **superseded** by the numbers below. The comparison tables `E_pair.md`, `E_forms.md` use the exact two-loop shift.
