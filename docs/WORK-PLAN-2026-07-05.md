## 2. The physics of the wall (why it is hard, and where it bites)

The mass gap is **dimensional transmutation**: a classically scale-invariant theory
generates a physical scale `Λ_YM` (hence a mass) non-perturbatively. On the lattice
one must send `a→0` while tuning the bare coupling `g(a)→0` (asymptotic freedom) so
that `Λ_YM = a^{-1}exp(−1/(2β₀g²))` stays fixed. The **lattice** gap `m_lat(a)→0`,
but the **physical** gap `Δ(a) = m_lat(a)/a` must stay uniformly in `[Δ_min, Δ_max]`
with `Δ_min > 0`. That spacing-uniform survival *is* `UniformSpacingFloor`.

Two consequences that must drive the plan:

- **The gap is non-perturbative** (`∼ exp(−1/g²)`). This is *proved to bite* in the
  Lean: the character/perturbative clustering mechanism provably caps at
  `w = 2^{−1/3}` (`ρ = 2w³ ≥ 1` beyond it — `character_window_excludes_weak`, a
  no-go theorem). Any attack on the weak/continuum region that is perturbative or
  character-diagonal **cannot** reach it. The continuum gap must be attacked with a
  genuinely non-perturbative object (RG flow of a polymer/cluster expansion, or a
  Balaban–Magnen–Rivasseau–Sénéor-class multiscale bound).
- **Mass gap ≠ area law.** The correct open analytic input is *spacing-uniform
  correlation decay* (`UniformPhysicalCorrelationDecay` / `ContinuumGapPersistence`),
  not a Wilson-loop area law. Confinement is a *separate* premise. Keep these
  uncoupled — the gap rides on correlation decay through the compact transfer
  operator, never an unbounded spectral theorem.
